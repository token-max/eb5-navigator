The visa bulletin, and the two clocks

Two deadlines are running and they are not the same deadline. One is statutory and fixed; the other moves every month and is published by a different department. Most of the urgency in the market is about the first. The second is the one that decides whether the green card is applied for from inside the country.

Read live at travel.state.gov and uscis.gov on 5 September 2026.

30 September 2026 — the statutory cliff

8 U.S.C. 1153(b)(5)(S)

The I-526E must be filed on or before this date. The protection is written into the statute: DHS “shall continue processing petitions … filed on or before September 30, 2026”, “may not deny a petition … based on the expiration of such legislation”, and “may not suspend or terminate the allocation of visas to the beneficiaries of approved petitions”. This date does not move and no bulletin affects it.

Whatever month the I-485 is received

8 U.S.C. 1255(n) · 8 CFR 245.1(a)

Adjustment of status can be filed with the petition rather than after it — but only if a visa is available. The statute allows the filing “whether the application is submitted concurrently with, or subsequent to, the visa petition”, and the regulation sets the test: a visa must be “immediately available at the time of filing of the application”. Availability is read off the visa bulletin for the month the filing is received.

Why the second clock is the one that decides the route

Those two sentences are the whole reason the second clock matters. Filing the I-485 alongside the petition is what keeps the case inside the United States. File the petition alone and the adjustment application waits on a visa bulletin nobody controls; if the category is not current when he is finally in a position to file, the remaining route to a green card is an immigrant visa interview at a consulate abroad.

The risk of the set-aside going non-current

Could the rural set-aside stop being current in October? Nobody outside the State Department knows, and it does not pre-announce set-aside cut-offs. What can be said is what was measured.

Points against a retrogression in October

1 October is a reset, not a squeeze. The annual limits start again, which is why categories usually improve at the start of a fiscal year — India unreserved went from a cut-off in September 2025 to a fresh date on 1 October 2025. The rural set-aside was current for every country in every month checked. And the State Department’s own warning in the September bulletin names one category: sufficient demand “in the EB-5 unreserved visa category” may make it necessary to retrogress or make it unavailable. No such warning was issued for any set-aside.

Points towards one, later

The arithmetic is now measured and it is not close. There are 2,646 rural visas a year worldwide, and USCIS received 2,122 rural petitions in the first half of FY2026 alone — 80% of a full year’s visas, counting investors only, with no family members and before the deadline rush. India’s own share annualises to roughly two thirds of the entire global pool. A set-aside that has never had a cut-off is not one that cannot. The full working is in the next section.

What the grandfathering does not do

The statutory protection is against the programme expiring, not against the annual numbers running out. It stops DHS refusing a petition because the regional centre legislation lapsed. It does not make a visa available in a month when the category is backed up, and it is administered by a different agency from the one that publishes the bulletin. Treating one as insurance against the other is the most expensive available misreading.

What changes if it does

If the rural set-aside acquires a cut-off date, what actually changes? It depends entirely on which side of the filing it happens.

It retrogresses after both forms are filed

Nothing changes about the filing. The regulation tests availability “at the time of filing”, so a properly filed I-485 stays filed. It cannot be approved until a number is available again, so the wait gets longer — but the case stays inside the country, and the work and travel documents that come with a pending application keep being renewable. This is the outcome the concurrent filing buys.

It retrogresses before the I-485 is filed

The petition can still be filed by 30 September and is still grandfathered; only the adjustment application is blocked. The statute expressly allows filing it “subsequent to” the petition, so the case is not lost — it is waiting, and the wait has to be spent in valid status inside the United States. The exposure is therefore not the bulletin, it is the runway on the current status.

It retrogresses and the runway runs out

This is the one outcome that defeats the whole objective. With no lawful status to wait in and no adjustment application on file, the remaining route to the green card is an immigrant visa interview at a consulate abroad. Every step below exists to make sure this branch is never reached.

What has to be true before it

What has to be true before the October bulletin, in order. The first four are what make a concurrent filing possible at all; the rest are what make it survive.

  1. Choose the project, and choose a reserved category rather than unreserved.
  2. Sign the subscription documents and wire the full amount, fee included.
  3. Have the source-of-funds record complete before filing, not after.
  4. File the I-485 in the same package as the I-526E, not after it.
  5. Complete the medical examination with a civil surgeon and include it.
  6. File the work and travel applications alongside, since both take months.
  7. Confirm how long the current status runs, because that is the real deadline behind the deadline.
  8. Check the bulletin the week of filing, because the month of receipt is what governs.
What is quoted, and what is not established

Every legal sentence above is quoted from the statute, the regulation or the bulletin itself, each named next to the point it supports, and all of them were read today rather than recalled. What is not established: whether the State Department will impose a rural cut-off in October, how large the post-deadline demand actually is, and how the per-country limits interact with the set-asides. So why is it still current?

Because a filing is not yet demand. The bulletin allocates numbers to “documentarily qualified applicants” — and this month it allocated against “demand received by August 10th”. A petition filed today is not documentarily qualified until it is approved, which takes a year or more. The wave measured above has been filed but not yet adjudicated, so it has not reached the bulletin yet. It is not a different phenomenon from the bulletin — it is the same one, arriving late.

What that lag means for the timing of a filing

The window is open because the wave has not landed

Rural is C today for the same reason it is oversubscribed on paper: the petitions exist but have not been approved. That gap is the window in which an I-485 can be filed from inside the country, and it closes when adjudication catches up, not when demand appears.

Position in the queue is set by filing date, permanently

When a cut-off date does appear, it is “the priority date of the first applicant who could not be reached within the numerical limits” — and the priority date is the day the I-526E was filed. Everyone who files after him is behind him, for good. Within the September rush, a filing on the 10th outranks the same filing on the 29th.

The surge measured here is the part before the surge

These are receipts to March 2026. The grandfathering deadline is 30 September 2026, and the whole market is being sold on it — one sponsor’s site carries a countdown clock on every page. The half-year already claims 80% of a year’s rural visas; the six months since are not in the data yet.

Two unknowns, both of which make it worse

Two things could not be established, and both make the picture worse rather than better. First, the family multiplier: every figure above counts investors, but a visa is consumed by the investor and by each accompanying spouse and child. The State Department report that would give the real ratio is blocked from here, so it is not estimated — which means every ratio above is a floor, not a projection. Second, whether the 7% per-country limit is applied inside a set-aside. The bulletin refers to “pro-rated per-country limits” and says a “foreign state in which demand was excessive” is deemed oversubscribed, so it can be. If it is, India’s share of those 2,646 is a fraction of India’s demand. That is the question for counsel, and it is the one that decides how bad this gets.

What is actually negotiable

Derived, not asserted. A term is negotiable here where materially similar deals in this same set carry materially different values for it — and on two of these the sponsor has the paperwork already drafted. Where a term does not vary, it is called fixed in this sample, which is fourteen deals and not a market.

The administrative fee — and two sponsors have the paperwork already drafted

This is the hardest evidence in the file. One sponsor supplied, in its own investor folder, a pre-drafted side-letter template that takes the administrative fee to US$0 — a form with bracketed placeholders for date, name and email, not a one-off. Its asking price is the coupon: 2.0% down to 0.5%. Break-even is $80,000 ÷ $12,000 a year = 6.67 years, so on the four-year headline term the trade is worth about +$32,000 — but the party holding the extension switch is the sponsor, which can extend an unlimited number of times, so the counterparty controls the only variable that decides whether the trade pays. Separately, another sponsor’s own offering memorandum lets the manager modify the fee by side letter and says expressly that it need not disclose the existence or terms of any side letter to other investors. Other people are getting discounts nobody will tell you about. Ask for one, in writing, before subscribing.

The return rate — it varies twentyfold across otherwise identical structures

0.25% on five funds, 2.0% on one, 5.0% on another — and the 5.0% is the equity tranche of the same project whose loan tranche pays 0.25%, from the same sponsor, on the same asset, in the same month. A term that moves 475 basis points between two tranches of one deal is a term, not a market. Ask what rate is available, and ask for it to accrue from the date of your wire rather than from deployment.

What the return accrues on, and when the clock starts

This is worth more than the headline rate and nobody negotiates it. As drafted, the rate runs only on the money actually advanced to the borrower, only from the date it is advanced, and fund administration costs are deducted from it “but not below 0.0%”. Ask for accrual on the full $880,000 from the date of the wire. That one change is worth more than doubling the stated rate.

When the term starts — and it is almost never the day you pay

Two funds run the clock from deployment to the borrower, not from your subscription. One matures on a fixed calendar date, 31 December 2028 — subscribe in September 2026 and the initial term is about 27 months, not three years. Another runs from a loan agreement dated 25 February 2026, months before you would sign anything. Ask for the maturity to be expressed as a period from your capital contribution.

Who owns the extension — currently, not you

Extensions belong to the borrower or the sponsor on every fund here. One can be extended an unlimited number of times at the lender’s option, where the lender is the fund the sponsor controls. Ask for a hard outside date, or for the coupon to step up on each extension. A step-up is the ask most likely to be granted, because it costs the sponsor nothing unless they are late.

Whether the fee is refunded if the petition is denied — currently it is not

On every fund that discloses it, the $80,000 is non-refundable in every scenario, including a full I-526E denial with the $800,000 returned. One says so in terms: “Notwithstanding a situation where the Investing Member is entitled to a full refund of his or her Capital Contribution… the Expense Amounts shall be non-refundable.” So the floor loss on a denial is $80,000 plus government and legal fees, even where the guaranty performs perfectly. Ask for the fee to be held in escrow until the I-526E is receipted, or refunded on denial.

Do not spend a question on these

Not negotiable, and do not spend a question on them: the $800,000 itself, the ten-jobs test, the sustainment period and the reserved-category rules are all statute. The escrow release trigger is usually set by an agreement with a bank that is already executed.

Immigration machinery

NCEnew commercial enterprise
The fund he buys a unit in. His petition names it, and it builds nothing itself.
JCEjob-creating entity
The company that actually spends the money, and whose spending the jobs are counted from.
I-526E
His own investor petition. It must be received by USCIS on or before 30 September 2026.
I-956F
The sponsor's application for the project itself. Approved, it binds USCIS when his own petition is read — subject to five carve-outs.
I-797notice of action
The paper USCIS sends when it approves a form. “I-797 in hand” on this page means the project’s actual approval notice has been read — not that a sponsor said one exists.
I-829
The petition filed two years later that turns the conditional green card into a permanent one. A job shortfall kills him here, not earlier.
I-485
The application that makes him a permanent resident from inside the US, so he never sits for a consular interview. Only a reserved category lets him file it alongside the I-526E.
I-797 / I-797C
The notices USCIS sends. An I-797 with "Approval" on its face is the approval; an I-797C is only a receipt. Read the notice type.
I-956G
The regional centre's annual report to USCIS about itself — the only regular filing in which it reports on its own conduct.
I-956H
The disclosures every person controlling a regional centre must file about their own record.
Conditional green card
The first green card, valid two years. The I-829 is what makes it permanent.
Priority date
The day USCIS receives his petition. It fixes his place in every queue after that — visa numbers, and in most of these funds, jobs.
Set-aside / reserved category
20% of EB-5 visas are held for rural projects, 10% for high-unemployment ones. The unreserved category is closed to an India-born applicant who wants to stay in the US.
TEAtargeted employment area
The designation that makes the price $800,000 rather than $1,050,000. Separate from the set-aside, and it does not decide his queue.
HUA
USCIS's own name for the 10% high-unemployment set-aside.
Visa Bulletin
The State Department's monthly notice of which visa queues are open. Published mid-month for the following month.
Final Action chart
The half of the Visa Bulletin that says whether a green card is actually available. UNAVAILABLE means he cannot file.
Cut-off date
A date on that chart. If one appears for India in his category before he files, he can no longer file the I-485 alongside the petition.

Two scores, never averaged

Capital risk asks whether the money comes back. Petition risk asks whether the green card arrives. They are governed by different bodies of law and only one of them has a financial regulator. A project can repay every dollar and still fail the job test; it can create every job and still lose the money. Blending them into one number hides exactly the thing worth knowing.

Higher is better on both, and the bar fills as the number rises. 65 would be a project with nothing left to find. 31 is the best capital number on this board, and it is not a good number — read the scale as “how much has survived checking”, not as a grade.

The two badges on the numbers

measured means the figure was read straight off a document or a government file. estimated means it was computed here from figures in those documents, and it is marked so you can re-run the arithmetic. Estimated does not mean doubtful — it is often more rigorous than the sponsor’s own rounded number. It means arithmetic rather than quotation.

Why so many “open” rather than “fail”

52 of the 112 gate cells on this board are open — 46% — against 3 outright failures. That is the finding, not a reassurance: almost nothing has been disproved because almost nothing has been shown. An open gate is a document nobody has produced, not an answer that came back badly. Read the open count, not the pass count.

The one that passes most gates is still not the best deal

Four projects now clear seven of these eight, and one of them — Moo Dairy — is a reject. Gates measure paperwork, not quality. They test whether the thing that must be true has been written down somewhere a reader can check, and a project can pass every one of them and still be a bad place for the money. Read them alongside the two scores, never instead of them. Where a project passes few gates it is usually because nobody has produced the documents, not because the answers came back badly — an unanswered question is not a failed one.

What each gate is actually testing

G1Set-aside categoryIs it rural, high-unemployment or infrastructure? An India-born applicant cannot file against the unreserved category at all — so this is not a preference, it decides whether the project is usable.
G2I-956F approvedUSCIS has adjudicated the project itself and the approval binds the agency at both petition stages, subject to five carve-outs. Without it, nobody has ever checked the project.
G3Regional centre liveExact-match the centre by ID and separately by name against both USCIS lists. Sponsors appear on both lists under different IDs, so an ID-only search returns a false all-clear.
G4No investor-triggerable repayment rightIf the investor can demand the money back, the capital is not “at risk” and the petition fails — even if the right is never exercised.

What settles it → Read the entity carefully, because it is not the one that owes you money. The respondent is the developer and job-creating entity. The borrower is the separate Florida company KH Cresswind Spring Haven UB LLC and the guarantor is TKG Property Holdings LLC; neither is a party, and a party search on the shared name prefix returns exactly one row. What was actually declared is unknown — guest access exposes no document text for any of the 25 entries. The entity whose jobs the green card depends on losing a contested ruling is worth understanding before filing a petition built on it.

What happens when an EB-5 project fails

Every offering document describes a plan. This is the only one in this research where the ending is already on the public record — a fund of 99 investors at $800,000 each, secured by a recorded first mortgage on 1,377 acres, whose borrower filed for bankruptcy. It is worth more than any brochure, because it shows what the protections actually do.

Everything below is quoted from two court documents: the debtor’s sworn schedules, filed 22 August 2025, and the settlement agreement the court approved on 24 March 2026. No sponsor supplied either one and neither can be edited by anyone with an interest in how it reads.

This was the structure you are told to want

Not a mezzanine slice, not preferred equity, not an unsecured note. A loan agreement dated 11 August 2022 for up to $79,200,000, funded by up to 99 investors at $800,000 each. Security was a Deed of Trust, Assignment of Rents and Leases, Security Agreement and Fixture Filing, executed 31 October 2022 and recorded on 9 November 2022 in two counties — Summit County entry 01197541, book 2763, page 1333, and Morgan County entry 162312, book 396, page 599. The collateral ran to twenty-three Summit County parcels and six in Morgan County, taking in the land, the buildings, the rents and the water rights. The debtor’s own filing lists the EB-5 fund first in priority. If you had read this offering in 2022 and checked the recorder, you would have found exactly what you were promised.

What happened anyway

  1. Nov 2022 – Nov 2024

    Money goes out the door across two years.

  2. 13 May 2025

    After sequential notices of default, the fund accelerates: the loan is terminated, no further money will be advanced, everything is due now.

  3. 8 August 2025

    The borrower files Chapter 11 in the District of Utah.

  4. 11 September 2025

    The fund moves on an emergency basis to have a trustee appointed, i.e. to take the company out of its own management’s hands. The court grants it on 25 September.

  5. 14 November 2025

    The trustee asks the court to let the fund lend the estate new money — up to $6.3m, later raised to $6.8m — including to pay a public infrastructure district assessment.

  6. 27 January 2026

    The trustee gives notice that he intends to challenge the fund’s own claim.

  7. 17–24 March 2026

    Private mediation. It settles on the last day.

What the fund actually got

Not money. Land. The settlement has the fund bidding for the borrower’s assets with the debt itself — a credit bid of $65,000,000, made up of the new loan first (about $7,000,000) and then $58,000,000 of the original claim, with the right to go up to $89,000,000 at auction. It adds $1,800,000 in cash to pay the administrative costs of the bankruptcy. The shortfall — the difference between the $86.2m owed and what it bids — is capped by agreement at $10,000,000 and ranks behind nothing but ahead of nothing, as an ordinary unsecured claim.

Where it leaves the investors

So the end state is this: ninety-nine people’s $78.87m has become a part-built golf and residential development in Utah that the fund now owns and has to finish, market and sell. There is no repayment date, because there is no repayment. There is a disposal.

What this teaches that no offering document will

1
A first lien buys control, not repayment.

The fund did everything a secured lender is supposed to do, in the right order and quickly: notices of default, acceleration, an emergency application for a trustee, rescue financing, a credit bid. The machinery worked exactly as designed. It still ends with the fund owning the land instead of holding the cash. When a sponsor says “we hold a first mortgage”, that is true and it is worth having — it is just not the same sentence as “you get your money back”.

2
Defending the lien cost roughly $13m of new money.

Up to $6.8m of rescue lending, $1.8m of cash at closing, a $4m guarantee to the other creditors backed by a standby letter of credit, and up to $400,000 to stop the golf course deteriorating. None of that was in the $79.2m the investors subscribed. A fund that cannot write this cheque loses the collateral to somebody who can. Ask every sponsor: if the borrower defaults, who funds the enforcement, out of what, and does drawing it dilute me? It is not a question any offering document answers on its own.

3
The trustee attacked the EB-5 fund’s claim itself.

Before mediation the trustee set out claims against the fund for constructive fraudulent transfer, equitable subordination, recharacterisation and unjust enrichment — and the settlement records, in terms, that these “could impact” the claim. Recharacterisation means a court treating what you called a loan as equity. Equitable subordination means pushing a secured lender behind the trade creditors it was ahead of. Both were live enough to settle. The fund bought a release, and the price shows in the gap between the $86.2m claim and the $58m it bid. This is the standing risk to every loan-model EB-5 deal and it is almost never disclosed as one.

4
A district assessment got paid before the mortgage.

The very first rescue-financing motion asked for authority to pay the local public infrastructure district’s 2025 assessment, and the settlement obliges the fund to take on all of that district’s obligations along with the taxes. An assessment is not a mortgage and does not queue behind one — it is collected like a property tax. If a project you are looking at carries a C-PACE loan, a special assessment, an improvement district or a special tax, that money sits ahead of yours in practice whatever the mortgage says. One project in this file carries a $100m C-PACE disclosure; this is the evidence for why that matters, and it is evidence rather than theory.

5
Four years in, and the clock is still running.

First money out in November 2022. Acceleration May 2025. Settlement March 2026. A sale in mid-2026 at the earliest, and then a development to build and sell after that. For the immigration side this is survivable and even helpful — capital that is demonstrably still at risk is exactly what the second petition needs. For a person who planned around getting the money back in year five, it is not.

What to put to a sponsor because of this

  1. If the borrower defaults, what is the source of enforcement capital, is it committed in writing, and does drawing on it dilute or subordinate me?
  2. Has any trustee, receiver or creditors’ committee ever asserted recharacterisation or equitable subordination against a fund you manage or have managed? Name every one.
  3. Does the collateral sit behind any assessment district, C-PACE lien, special tax or improvement district? Name each and state the balance outstanding.
  4. What is the current loan-to-value on the collateral, on whose appraisal, and dated when?
  5. Does the fund’s governing document let the manager commit further investor capital to protect the lien without a vote?

This is one fund and one borrower, and a bad outcome for one project is not a verdict on a sponsor or on the programme. It is used here for the opposite reason: it is the only place in this research where the machinery can be watched working, so it is the only place the abstract protections can be priced. Read it as a specimen, not as an indictment.
